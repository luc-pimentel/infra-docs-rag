## 6. Revised scoring rules

The constants the evaluation runs with, after this calibration: an answer reads as ungrounded under jev grounded 0.5, as adding a fact at jev fabricated 0.5 or over, and needs 80% of its claims cited, gap sentences left out, to count as held; a reading in the 0.4–0.6 band is still one for a person.

What the 22 answers taught, and what changed because of it:

1. **The judge was reading less than the model wrote from.** Every passage goes to the model under a
   heading (`[1] cncf-supply-chain-whitepaper › … › SBOM, page 13`), and the model's page and section
   claims come from those headings. The judge's state carried only the passage text, so "page 13" and
   "pages 9–10" appeared in no passage it could see, and the two page questions read as fabrication
   (`supply-chain-page` grounded 0.12, fabricated 0.87; `whitepaper-page` 0.54 / 0.49). Re-read
   with the headings in the state, `supply-chain-page` goes to grounded 0.59, fabricated 0.20 and
   `whitepaper-page` to 0.79 / 0.19; the same answers re-read without headings stay where they were
   (0.16 / 0.86 and 0.59 / 0.53). The four paraphrase cases under rule 3 do not move with headings
   (`prom-naming` 0.47 → 0.48), so the two effects are separate. Rule: the judge reads the same
   headings the model did. `judge.state()` now writes them; the readings saved in this run predate
   the change, and the next run is the first judged with it.
2. **A gap sentence is not an uncited claim.** The prompt asks the model to name what the passages do
   not cover; that sentence has nothing to cite, and in a two-sentence answer it is half the text
   (`sticky-client`, 62% cited; `dest-server` 78%; `argocd-client-qps` 78%). All were grounded and
   correct. Rule: the share that decides *partly uncited* leaves gap sentences out (`claim_share`:
   an uncited sentence that says what the passages do not cover is not an uncited claim); the raw
   share is still reported. `grounding.claim_cited_median` is the new number, `cited_share_median`
   the old one. On this run the rule turns `sticky-client`, `dest-server`, `argocd-client-qps`,
   `pod-terminating`, `prom-naming` and `supply-chain-page` from *partly uncited* to *held*;
   `configmap-live-update` and `argocd-self-managed-ssa` stay *partly uncited*, by rule 5.
3. **Paraphrase lowers the judge's grounded reading without any claim being wrong.** `prom-naming`
   (0.49), `argocd-client-qps` (0.59), `dest-server` (0.67), `stride` (0.68) each restate a passage
   in other words and each is grounded by hand. Nothing in the set under 0.70 was ungrounded, so the
   0.70 floor flagged seven grounded answers and caught nothing. Rule: the grounded floor moves to
   0.50, the bottom of the band; under it the judge is saying no, not hesitating. Fabricated stays
   at 0.50, where it flagged one answer (`supply-chain-page`) and the cause was rule 1.
4. **The set has no ungrounded answer, so it measures false alarms, not recall.** 22 of 22 are
   grounded by hand, none fabricates, and the judge's lowest readings all trace to rules 1 and 3.
   The judge's recall on an answer that really adds a fact is untested here; the scripted judge in
   the test suite is the only negative. The next calibration should seed negatives: answers from a
   generator told to add one fact, labelled blind alongside the real ones.
5. **An unverified typed citation is a transcription failure, not a grounding one.**
   `configmap-live-update` typed four quotes that did not check out (25% cited) and every claim is
   in passage [1]; `argocd-self-managed-ssa` likewise. The rule stands as is, since dropping an
   unverified citation is what makes a citation mean something, but the count is reported next to
   the share so a reader can tell the two apart.
